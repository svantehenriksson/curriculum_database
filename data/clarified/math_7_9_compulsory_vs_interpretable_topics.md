## B) National-Level: Possibly Compulsory / Open to Interpretation

These topics are likely needed in many implementations, but the exact compulsory depth can be interpreted differently across local curricula.

1. How formal mathematical proof must be (beyond introductory idea)  
2. How deep truth-value logic should go (simple testing vs stronger formal logic)  
3. Programming depth (short scripts vs larger tasks)  
4. Software use depth (calculator level vs dynamic graphing workflows)  
5. How far polynomial techniques should extend  
6. How far simple quadratic solving should extend  
7. Graph interpretation depth for functions in mixed contexts  
8. Required sophistication of geometric constructions  
9. Required depth for trigonometric problem types  
10. Required depth for circle theorems and theorem naming  
11. How much 3D surface-area/volume complexity is required  
12. Whether unit conversion includes complex multi-step contexts  
13. Whether sampling bias and uncertainty are explicitly required or only implied  
14. Whether probability includes only single-step events or also multi-step settings  
15. The amount of real-life modeling expected from students  
16. The expected level of mathematical communication quality  
17. The expected depth of strategic problem-solving heuristics  
18. The expected depth of self-monitoring and reflective mathematical work

## C) Municipality Follow-Up (From Current Sample)

Current local sample in evidence:

- Abo local 2026 (SV)
- Helsingfors local 2016 (SV)
- Helsinki local 2016 (FI)
- Kirkkonummi local 2025 (FI)
- Kyrkslatt local 2026 (SV)
- Turku local 2026 (FI)
- Vaasa local (FI)
- Vasa local (SV)

### What is very consistent across municipalities

Across this sample, the most consistently explicit clusters are:

- Algebraic expressions and expression work  
- Fractions/decimals  
- Percentage calculations  
- Functions and proportionality (usually at least partly explicit)  
- Basic geometry lines/angles (often explicit)

### Where municipalities differ most

1. **How explicit they are in grade-by-grade wording**  
   Some curricula list many concrete items per grade; others stay high-level.

2. **How much they name advanced subtopics directly**  
   Topics like equation systems, inequalities, or specific geometry theorem use are very explicit in some local texts and much lighter in others.

3. **How strongly they foreground digital tools/programming**  
   Present in several local curricula, but level and frequency vary.

4. **How much “method” content is explicit**  
   Reasoning, communication, self-regulation, and evaluation appear unevenly as explicit text.

### Practical read of local profiles in this sample

- **Abo, Kyrkslatt, Turku**: comparatively detailed and topic-explicit grade-level wording.  
- **Helsinki, Helsingfors**: broad objective structure is visible; many concrete content details may remain less explicit in extracted rows.  
- **Kirkkonummi**: concise but clear local statements; many topics are present with less granularity than the most detailed curricula.  
- **Vaasa, Vasa**: compact topic wording; strongest for broad content categories, less explicit fine-grain decomposition.

## D) Working Interpretation for Your Textbook Planning

For a 7-9 textbook structure, the safest planning rule is:

- Treat the **Compulsory Core Topics** list above as the required national baseline.
- Treat the **Possibly Compulsory / Open to Interpretation** list as content to include in a flexible way (optional depth blocks, municipality-specific extensions, or teacher-selectable modules).

This gives you one nationally aligned core while still respecting local variation in explicitness and depth.
